Model comparison spec 6.2, 6.3

Accuracy is the wrong metric to select on

A trivial model that always predicts "No Failure" scores 93.08% accuracy and catches zero real failures. Random Forest reaches 94.87% accuracy yet its recall is only 37.18% — it still misses almost two in three real failures. This is why spec 6.3 requires selection on Failure-class Precision/Recall/F1, never on accuracy.

One axis, 0–100%. Why Logistic Regression over Random Forest

Random Forest's plain variant has the second-highest accuracy (94.87%) of all six candidates, but its Failure F1 (0.5012) is the worst of the three plain (non-balanced) models — it is good at the easy 93% of cases and poor at the 7% that matter operationally. Logistic Regression's plain variant has both the highest accuracy (96.95%) and the highest Failure F1 (0.7589) of all six candidates, so it wins on the criterion spec 6.3 actually names, without even needing the accuracy/F1 trade-off the balanced variants make.
